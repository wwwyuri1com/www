"...I believe I do."

"Wonderful."

The Queen smiled.

"Then I look forward to seeing how the future unfolds."

Yulina's face was now entirely red.

Silene's ears were not in much better condition.

The King, meanwhile, appeared to have decided that some diplomatic matters were best left to his wife.

The Western Garden

By the time they left the audience chamber, the afternoon sun was beginning to set.

Yulina walked beside Silene.

Neither spoke.

They passed through the western corridor.

Then the garden.

Then beneath a flowering arbor.

Finally—

Yulina stopped.

"...Silene."

"Yes, Your Highness?"

A pause.

"...What just happened?"

Silene considered the question.

Then answered with perfect seriousness.

"I believe Their Majesties are considering amendments to the royal charter."

Yulina stared at her.

"...That's not what I meant!"

Silene's mouth curved.

"I suspected as much."

Yulina covered her face.

"Oh, this is unbearable..."

The captain smiled faintly.

For a while, they stood beneath the arbor.

Then Yulina lowered her hands.

"...Silene?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think it's strange?"

"What is?"

"The idea of having a Queen and a female King."

Silene considered the question.

"Unusual, certainly."

"..."

"But not unreasonable."

Yulina looked up.

"Really?"

"Yes."

Silene glanced toward the palace.

"A title is ultimately a responsibility. If the law permits it, I see no reason why a capable woman should be excluded."

Yulina smiled faintly.

"...You make everything sound so simple."

"Not everything."

"Oh?"

Silene looked back at her.

"Some matters are considerably more complicated."

Yulina's heartbeat quickened.

"...Such as?"

The captain hesitated.

Then answered quietly.

"Knowing whether one is permitted to wish for something."

Yulina went still.

For a moment, neither spoke.

A soft breeze moved through the garden.

"...Silene."

"Yes?"

Yulina looked down at her hands.

Then, gathering her courage—

"Would you ever consider it?"

Silene blinked.

"...Consider what?"

Yulina's cheeks turned pink.

"...Becoming King."

Silence.

Silene stared.

Then she looked away.

For the first time in Yulina's memory, the captain appeared genuinely uncertain how to answer.

"...That is a rather extraordinary question, Your Highness."

"I know."

"A position of immense responsibility."

"Yes."

"One that would require years of preparation."

"I know."

"And one that should never be pursued lightly."

Yulina nodded.

"...I know."

Silene looked at her.

Then asked gently—

"May I ask why you're asking me?"

Yulina froze.

"..."

Her face grew redder.

"...Well."

Silene waited.

"...Because."

"Because?"

Yulina looked desperately toward the garden.

There were flowers.

There were trees.

There was a perfectly ordinary stone fountain.

Unfortunately, none of them appeared capable of rescuing her from this conversation.

"...Because I think you'd make a very good King."

Silene became quiet.

Then smiled faintly.

"That is a generous assessment."

"It's true."

"Your Highness—"

"And I think you'd look wonderful wearing a crown."

Silene blinked.

"...A crown?"

Yulina immediately regretted speaking.

"THAT'S NOT THE IMPORTANT PART!"

Silene laughed.

"Of course."

"The important part is that you're reliable!"

"Yes."

"And intelligent!"

"Thank you."

"And honorable!"

"You're very kind."

"And..."

Yulina stopped.

Her voice grew smaller.

"...And I would like having you beside me."

Silene's smile faded into something gentler.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then—

"Yulina."

The princess looked up.

Silene continued quietly.

"If I ever sought such a position, it would not be for the crown."

"..."

"Nor for the title."

"..."

"And certainly not because Their Majesties suggested it."

Yulina listened.

Silene took a slow breath.

"It would have to be because I wished to stand beside the person who wore it."

The princess's heart gave a tremendous leap.

"...Oh."

Silene smiled.

"But that is not a decision I should make today."

Yulina nodded quickly.

"Of course!"

"Nor should you."

"Absolutely!"

"We have much to learn about each other."

"Yes!"

"And you have considerable responsibilities ahead of you."

"I know!"

Silene paused.

Then smiled.

"...And I suspect I have some rather formidable competition."

Yulina blinked.

"...Competition?"

"Surely there will be many remarkable women interested in becoming the royal consort."

Yulina stared.

"..."

Silene looked entirely serious.

"Scholars. Diplomats. Military officers. Perhaps even foreign princesses."

"..."

"Such a position would undoubtedly attract considerable attention."

Yulina's expression slowly changed.

"...Silene."

"Yes?"

"Are you telling me that you're worried about competition?"

Silene paused.

"..."

Yulina stepped a little closer.

"Captain?"

"...Your Highness."

"Are you?"

Silene looked away.

And her ears turned pink.

Yulina's eyes widened.

Then a delighted smile spread across her face.

"...Oh."

Silene sighed.

"...Please don't."

"OH!"

"Your Highness."

"YOU ARE!"

Silene closed her eyes.

Yulina laughed.

For several seconds, she could barely contain herself.

Then, still smiling, she asked—

"...Would you like to compete?"

Silene opened her eyes.

The question hung between them.

No royal command.

No political obligation.

No guaranteed appointment.

Just a woman asking another woman whether she wished to try.

Silene looked at Yulina.

At the princess's hopeful expression.

At her unmistakably pink cheeks.

At the smile she had begun looking forward to seeing every day.

Then she bowed her head slightly.

"...If the future Queen would permit it."

Yulina's breath caught.

Silene continued.

"I believe I would like to try."

For a moment, Yulina could only stare.

Then she smiled.

A small, brilliant smile.

"...Really?"

"Yes."

The princess clasped her hands behind her back.

Then tilted her head.

"Well, Captain Silene..."

The captain straightened.

"Yes, Your Highness?"

"I should warn you that the position may have rather demanding qualifications."

Silene raised an eyebrow.

"Indeed?"

"Yes."

Yulina began counting on her fingers.

"Exceptional loyalty."

"Naturally."

"Good judgment."

"Of course."

"Excellent swordsmanship."

"Understood."

"And..."

She hesitated.

"...A willingness to accompany the Queen on walks."

Silene smiled.

"That seems manageable."

"And drink tea with her."

"Certainly."

"And listen when she has something important to say."

"Always."

Yulina looked up.

"...And sometimes, perhaps, hold her hand."

Silene grew quiet.

For a moment, the garden seemed very still.

Then the captain slowly extended her hand.

"...Would that be part of the examination?"

Yulina stared.

Her cheeks turned scarlet.

"...Perhaps."

Silene smiled.

"Then I suppose I should begin preparing."

Yulina laughed softly.

And took her hand.

No promises of marriage were made.

No royal titles were granted.

And no decision concerning the kingdom's future King had yet been reached.

But as the two women walked through the western garden, hand in hand, Crown Princess Yulina found herself thinking that perhaps the future did not need to be decided all at once.

For now—

She had someone she wanted beside her.

And that someone, for the very first time, had chosen to walk toward her without being summoned.

Meanwhile, in the Royal Chambers

The King looked out through the window.

Far below, two familiar figures were walking through the garden.

One wore the elegant dress of a Crown Princess.

The other wore the dark uniform of the Royal Guard.

And—

They appeared to be holding hands.

The King stared.

"...Darling."

The Queen looked up from her book.

"Yes?"

"...I think Captain Silene is courting our daughter."

The Queen followed his gaze.

Then smiled.

"Oh?"

The King nodded gravely.

"Quite seriously, it seems."

"How wonderful."

A pause.

"...Should we do something?"

The Queen looked at him.

"Such as?"

"I don't know. Perhaps offer advice?"

"Advice?"

"Or establish a formal courtship procedure?"

The Queen laughed.

"Darling."

"Yes?"

"Why don't we leave them alone?"

The King considered this.

Then nodded.

"...Yes. You're right."

He watched the two figures disappear beneath the flowering arbor.

A small smile appeared on his face.

"...She really is an excellent captain."

The Queen chuckled.

"Indeed."

"Though I wonder whether she understands what she's getting herself into."

The Queen looked amused.

"Which one?"

The King paused.

Then laughed.

"...Both, I suppose."

The Queen closed her book.

"Well."

She smiled toward the garden.

"Then perhaps Asteria's future King will have to work rather hard for her crown."

The King nodded thoughtfully.

"...A worthy challenge."

A pause.

"Although..."

The Queen turned.

"Yes?"

"After what happened to Prince Adrian, I sincerely hope none of the other candidates challenge Captain Silene to a sword match."

The Queen burst out laughing.

"Oh, darling."

She reached for her tea.

"I rather suspect our daughter would enjoy that far too much."
