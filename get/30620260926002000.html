Whiter had dreamed about this job long before she ever graduated.

Not the office itself. Not the salary. Not even the fancy development labs.

The dog food.

She had a nutritionist's license, a degree that had taken years of studying, and absolutely no hesitation about spending all of it developing better food for dogs.

So when she landed a position in the product development department of a company specializing in dog food and canned meals, Whiter had considered it the greatest achievement of her young life.

She had also shown up to her first day wearing a red plaid shirt.

It was not technically part of the company dress code.

Nobody had the heart to tell her that.

With her waist-length golden hair, naturally fluffy and slightly wild, Whiter looked less like a nutritionist and more like someone who had wandered out of a forest after arguing with a wolf.

She loved dogs.

She especially loved her German Shepherd, Coffee.

And, unfortunately for everyone around her, Coffee was also involved in her professional life.

Officially, Whiter took prototype products home for palatability testing.

Unofficially, she took them home because Coffee liked them.

And because she liked them.

"All right, Coffee. New prototype."

Whiter placed a small bowl on the kitchen floor with the solemnity of a scientist presenting the results of a decade-long study.

Coffee immediately sat down.

His eyes locked onto the bowl.

Whiter crouched beside him.

"Remember. Professional evaluation."

Coffee's tail thumped against the floor.

"You smell it first."

His nose twitched.

"Then you take one bite."

Coffee leaned forward.

"One."

He ate the entire sample.

Whiter stared at the empty bowl.

"...That was fast."

Coffee looked up at her.

Whiter narrowed her eyes.

"Okay. Again."

She put another sample down.

Coffee devoured it.

Whiter glanced at her clipboard.

"Excellent appetite. Strong acceptance. No hesitation."

Coffee licked his lips.

Whiter nodded thoughtfully.

"Very promising."

Then she reached for the bowl.

Coffee immediately put one paw over it.

Whiter froze.

"...Coffee."

She stared back.

"That's my sample."

Coffee's paw remained firmly planted.

"You're the tester."

A low, suspicious growl.

Coffee pulled it back.

"Hey."

"Come on."

Another tug.

The bowl slid across the floor.

Whiter followed it on her knees.

"Coffee, I'm your big sister."

"You are literally eating something I developed."

Whiter's golden hair fell over her face.

"Give me the food."

Her expression changed.

"Oh."

"...Actually, this is pretty good."

Coffee stared.

Whiter stared back.

Then she grabbed the bowl.

"No."

Coffee stood up.

"This one's mine."

The following morning, Whiter arrived at the development department carrying a completely serious nutritional report.

She had written:

Prototype 27-B — Extremely High Acceptance Rate.

Underneath it, she had added:

Potential issue: Tester unwilling to share.

She had not specified which tester.

That was probably for the best.

The development department had another problem.

Its director.

Shelly Stellar.

Cold, composed, and almost frighteningly difficult to read, Shelly ran the department with the kind of precision that made everyone instinctively sit straighter whenever she walked past.

She was beautiful.

She was wealthy.

She knew it.

And she had spent enough years watching people approach her for one reason or another that she had developed a simple rule.

Anyone getting too close probably wanted something.

Money.

Status.

Her appearance.

Usually some combination of all three.

Romance was therefore not part of her life.

Neither was smiling at subordinates.

Especially not before coffee.

The employees had privately given her a nickname.

The Doberman.

It was never spoken anywhere near her.

Whiter, however, had only been there for three days.

She had absolutely no idea.

She was too busy carrying another prototype into the laboratory.

Shelly walked past her.

Whiter glanced over.

Their eyes met.

Shelly's expression remained perfectly neutral.

Whiter smiled.

"Good morning!"

Shelly stopped.

"...Good morning."

Then she continued walking.

Whiter watched her go.

"Huh."

She looked down at her sample container.

"She looks like Coffee when he doesn't want to share."

Across the room, three employees simultaneously looked at the ceiling.

Shelly stopped walking.

Slowly, she turned her head.

Whiter was already opening the container.

Shelly stared at her.

Whiter looked up.

"...Want to try some?"

The entire development department went silent.

Shelly's expression did not change.

"No."

Whiter nodded.

"Okay."

She turned back to her work.

Shelly continued staring for another second.

Then she walked away.

Behind her, someone quietly whispered,

"She's going to die."

Whiter looked around.

"Huh?"

Nobody answered.

She shrugged and returned to her prototype.

After all, it was only dog food.

How complicated could this job possibly become?
